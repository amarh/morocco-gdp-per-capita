Economic history · 1956–2022

Morocco since independence: richer, but outpaced

Between independence in 1956 and 2022, Morocco's real GDP per capita rose 3.6-fold, from $2,313 to $8,400 (2011 dollars at purchasing-power parity). That is 1.97% a year. Most economies that started at a similar level grew much faster, and Morocco slipped from 5th to 10th among twelve comparable countries.

Key findings

Six things the data show about Morocco's income per person since independence.

11th / 12

Slow compared with peers

Only Jordan grew more slowly among twelve emerging economies with similar income in the 1950s. The median peer grew 3.3% a year.

$19,300

The cost of the gap

Had Morocco grown at the peer median since 1956, its GDP per capita would be about $19,300 today, 2.3 times its actual level.

5th → 10th

Overtaken

South Korea, Thailand, Egypt, Indonesia and Tunisia all started below Morocco and ended above it. South Korea's income grew 30-fold.

≈14%

No catching up with rich economies

Morocco stayed at 13–14% of the US level. Against Spain it fell from 49% (1956) to 18% (2000), then recovered to 25% (2022).

3.8%

Growth came late

The 2000s were Morocco's best decade. Earlier, the 1980s debt crisis and 1990s droughts held growth near 1.3–1.4% a year.

15 years

A trend problem, not a shock problem

Income per person fell in 15 of 66 years, mostly droughts, but Morocco's volatility is average for the group. The gap comes from a lower trend.

Why it matters. 2.0% versus 3.3% a year looks small, but it compounds. At 2.0% income doubles every 35 years; at 3.3% every 22 years. The stronger growth since 2000 shows faster progress is achievable.

Is Morocco catching up?

Catching up means growing faster than richer countries so the gap narrows. Against the United States, Morocco's position barely changed. Against Spain, it lost ground until 2000 and has partly recovered since.

Method

  • Main measure: real GDP per capita in 2011 international dollars at purchasing-power parity, Maddison Project Database 2023 (1950–2022).
  • Second measure: World Bank GDP in current US dollars divided by population (1960–2023). Not adjusted for inflation; moves with exchange rates.
  • Growth is the compound annual rate between the years shown. Ranks are among the 12 emerging peers.
  • Peers: Maghreb (Algeria, Tunisia); Middle East (Egypt, Jordan, Türkiye); economies with similar 1950s income (South Korea, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia, Philippines, Vietnam). References: Spain, Portugal, United States.

Caveats

  • 1950s estimates are rough; Morocco's series is interpolated before the mid-1960s. Measured from 1960, Morocco's growth is 2.2% a year instead of 2.0%; the rankings do not change.
  • 1956 is Morocco's independence year; for Algeria (1962) and Malaysia (1957) it precedes their own.
  • PPP and exchange-rate measures can rank countries differently (Egypt is above Morocco at PPP, below it in US dollars after its devaluations).
  • The World Bank series here is an earlier release; the latest gives Morocco about $3,814 (2023) and $4,153 (2024).
